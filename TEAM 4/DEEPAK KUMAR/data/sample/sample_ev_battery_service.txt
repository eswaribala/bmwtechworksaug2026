SECTION 1: HIGH-VOLTAGE BATTERY OVERHEATING DIAGNOSIS

1.1 Symptom & Fault Overview
When an electric vehicle (EV) reports repeated high-voltage (HV) battery overheating alerts or fault code 21A004 / 21A008, technicians must immediately execute a systematic thermal diagnostic workflow before replacing components.

1.2 Recommended Diagnostic Procedures
When an EV reports repeated battery overheating, perform the following verification and inspection steps:
1. Coolant Circuit & Level Verification:
   - Check high-voltage battery cooling circuit fluid level in the secondary expansion reservoir.
   - Inspect the electric coolant pump V54 for proper speed feedback, power supply, and cavitation noise.
   - Perform a pressure leakage test on the battery cooling loop at 1.5 bar for 15 minutes.

2. Fault Code & Diagnostic Trouble Code (DTC) Readout:
   - Connect ISTA/D diagnostic interface and read fault codes from the Thermal Management Control Unit (TMCU) and Cell Monitoring Circuit (CMC).
   - Check for DTC 21A004 (HV Battery Temperature Exceeds Threshold > 55°C) and DTC 21A008 (Coolant Flow Rate Deficit).

3. Temperature Sensor & Cell Module Inspection:
   - Monitor live values for all 12 internal module NTC temperature sensors.
   - Verify temperature symmetry across modules 1 through 12. If a single module strays by > 8°C from the average, inspect the heat transfer thermal pad interface.

4. Heat Exchanger & Chiller Valve Operations:
   - Inspect the heat exchanger (chiller) shut-off valve Y102 for binding or electrical failure.
   - Verify refrigerant expansion valve Y104 operation during active battery cooling mode.

1.3 Safety Notice
Always de-energize the high-voltage system using the High-Voltage Disconnect Service Plug before servicing cooling line connectors or removing battery module covers.
